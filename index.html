2026-11-07 · Does the teacher's field of view matter in nuclear SSL? — a pre-registered three-arm answer

Controlled continued-training study (ViT-S/8 initialized from public nuclear-SSL weights, single-domain pool, identical budget and probe contracts) comparing three supervision contracts on the same 62k-cell pool: (a) no cross-view objective, (b) full-context (96px) teacher distillation, (c) a teacher restricted to the co-visible 40px region before encoding. Three task families (fine-grained nuclei typing, cross-domain typing, tile-level organ) with patient/tile-level paired bootstrap inference, two seeds, and an independent adversarial audit plus bit-level closure rerun.

  • Co-visible restriction is not necessary. The full-context teacher outperforms the restricted one by +2.96 pp bAcc [95% CI 1.92–3.99] on fine-grained typing — a clean content effect after confound exclusion (same 26 aligned target pairs, same reduction, budget-matched).
  • Full-context teacher adaptation is the only contract that beats the frozen anchor (+3.42 pp [2.40–4.42]). The gain sits almost entirely in low-margin cells (96.3% in the bottom margin quartile) and minority-class recovery.
  • The cross-domain cost is real but structured: a broad content-level probability shift concentrated on a pre-existing confusable family (one class macro-F1 −21.6 pp); it is not context leakage (0/64 false positives had any same-class neighbor), not regularization strength, and creates no new confusion modes across a 16-model audit.
  • Takeaway: at equal budget, target-distribution richness dominated view-restriction. A dose-response check (64/144px teachers) is drafted as the closing experiment.

Honest terms: continued-training adaptation (not from scratch); single-domain pool; TCGA-overlap in-domain tendencies on absolute numbers. Preprint with full contracts, audit trail and gate logic in preparation.
